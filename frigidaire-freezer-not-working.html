Frigidaire Freezer Not Working / Not Freezing

Quick answer: Three quick triage questions: Is the compressor running? Is there heavy frost on the back panel (defrost failure)? Is the condenser clean and its fan running? Those three forks cover most cases.

Safety first

  • Unplug the appliance before any inspection that goes beyond the exterior — capacitors and live terminals can hold a charge even when powered off.
  • Never bypass a safety device (thermal fuse, door switch, interlock). It opened for a reason; bypassing it creates a fire or shock hazard.
  • If you smell burning, see arcing, or water is contacting electrical parts, stop and call a professional.

Diagnosis path (in order)

  1. Listen for the compressor. A quiet freezer with no compressor hum and interior lights on points to the cold control, start relay/overload, or compressor itself. Click-then-silence repeating is the classic start-relay failure sound.
  2. Check the evaporator fan. With the door switch pressed (or door closed, ear close), you should hear the fan whenever the compressor runs. A silent fan means frozen-coil cooling never circulates — test the fan motor for continuity.
  3. Inspect the back wall for frost. Heavy frost/snow on the evaporator cover means a defrost failure: defrost thermostat, defrost heater, or timer/adaptive defrost control. Manually defrosting (24 h with the door open, towels down) restores operation temporarily and confirms the diagnosis.
  4. Clean the condenser and check its fan. Dust-choked condenser coils let the compressor overheat and short-cycle. Vacuum them (unplug first) and confirm the condenser fan spins freely.
  5. Check the door gasket. A dollar bill closed in the door should resist pulling all the way around. A leaking gasket passes humid air — frost builds fast and runtimes climb.
  6. Cools poorly but runs constantly. With clean coils and good airflow, constant-running-poor-cooling points to a sealed-system issue (leak/restriction) — a technician-level repair worth weighing against replacement on older units.

Tools you may need

Screwdrivers (Phillips and flat), a flashlight, and towels for water work. Electrical checks need a basic multimeter (continuity mode) — a $15–25 household model is sufficient [EST].

Model note [SYM]: Upright and chest configurations move components around; the model plate is usually inside on a side wall. Record the full number — Frigidaire made many variants under the same size classes.

Typical diagnosis time: Diagnosis 30–45 minutes (plus defrost wait if frost is found) [EST]

Frequently asked questions

Is it worth repairing or should I replace?

Rule of thumb: repair when the fix costs under about half of replacement and the appliance is under 8–10 years old [EST]. Confirm your exact model number first — it changes both the parts and the diagnosis.

Do I need a multimeter?

For any electrical check (fuses, elements, sensors, switches) yes. A basic continuity-capable meter is the single most useful appliance-repair tool [EST $15–25].

Where is my model number?

Washers/dryers: inside the door or lid rim. Refrigerators: inside a compartment wall. Ovens: behind the drawer or inside the door. Dishwashers: door rim. Record the full number before ordering parts.
