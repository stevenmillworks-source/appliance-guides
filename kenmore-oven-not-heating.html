Kenmore Oven Not Heating (or Not Heating Enough)

Quick answer: On electric models, the bake element is the usual failure: look for blisters or breaks, then confirm with a continuity test. Broil-works-bake-doesn't isolates it immediately. Gas models: a glowing-but-not-lighting igniter is still a failed igniter.

Safety first

  • Unplug the appliance before any inspection that goes beyond the exterior — capacitors and live terminals can hold a charge even when powered off.
  • Never bypass a safety device (thermal fuse, door switch, interlock). It opened for a reason; bypassing it creates a fire or shock hazard.
  • If you smell burning, see arcing, or water is contacting electrical parts, stop and call a professional.

Diagnosis path (in order)

  1. Read the model prefix first. Kenmore appliances were built by several manufacturers (110/Whirlpool, 417/Frigidaire, 665/Whirlpool-kitchen, 790/Frigidaire, 363/GE lineage). The prefix determines the actual platform and the correct parts.
  2. Electric: inspect the bake element. Blisters, bubbles, or a visible break in the coil confirm failure without a meter. Even unseen damage often shows as a rough or roughened patch.
  3. Test the element (power off).
  4. Broil works but bake does not. That pattern isolates the fault to the bake element or its relay on the control — the sensor and thermostat are shared, so they are fine.
  5. Neither element heats, display alive. Suspect the thermal cutoff (check continuity) or the electronic oven control. Check the wiring diagram inside the control panel.
  6. Temperature way off but it heats. Test the oven temperature sensor at room temperature — commonly around 1,080 Ω at 70 °F on many platforms, but verify the spec for your model. A drifted sensor produces under/over-cooking without a hard failure.
  7. Gas models: test the igniter. The igniter must draw enough current to open the safety valve. An igniter that glows but never lights the burner is still the failed part — weak current cannot open the valve. [EST $60–120 part]

Tools you may need

Screwdrivers (Phillips and flat), a flashlight, and towels for water work. Electrical checks need a basic multimeter (continuity mode) — a $15–25 household model is sufficient [EST].

Model note [SYM]: Kenmore model number is on a frame behind the drawer or inside the door. The three-digit prefix (106, 110, 362, 363, 417, 665, 790...) identifies the real manufacturer — record it before any parts search.

Typical diagnosis time: Diagnosis 20–40 minutes [EST]

Frequently asked questions

Is it worth repairing or should I replace?

Rule of thumb: repair when the fix costs under about half of replacement and the appliance is under 8–10 years old [EST]. Confirm your exact model number first — it changes both the parts and the diagnosis.

Do I need a multimeter?

For any electrical check (fuses, elements, sensors, switches) yes. A basic continuity-capable meter is the single most useful appliance-repair tool [EST $15–25].

Where is my model number?

Washers/dryers: inside the door or lid rim. Refrigerators: inside a compartment wall. Ovens: behind the drawer or inside the door. Dishwashers: door rim. Record the full number before ordering parts.
