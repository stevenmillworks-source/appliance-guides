Samsung Washer Error Codes (4C, 5C, SUD, Ub, dS, LE, 3E): Meanings & Fixes

Quick answer: These codes map to fill, drain, suds, balance, door-lock, and motor subsystems. 4C (water supply) and 5C (drain) are the most common — and both are usually installation or debris issues you can fix in under an hour.

Samsung washers report faults as two-to-three character codes. The code tells you which subsystem stopped responding — fill, drain, suds, balance, door lock, or motor. Most fill and drain codes are caused by installation issues or debris, not failed parts, which is why the checks below start there.

Safety first

  • Unplug the appliance before any inspection that goes beyond the exterior — capacitors and live terminals can hold a charge even when powered off.
  • Never bypass a safety device (thermal fuse, door switch, interlock). It opened for a reason; bypassing it creates a fire or shock hazard.
  • If you smell burning, see arcing, or water is contacting electrical parts, stop and call a professional.

What each code means and what to check

CodeMeaning, why it happens, and the checks (in order)
4C / 4E (also shown as nF)

Water supply error — the tub did not fill to the target level in time [SPEC].

The washer meters water with a pressure/level sensor. If flow is weak or absent, the sensor never sees the target level and the machine stops with 4C/4E rather than running a cycle with insufficient water.

  • Confirm both faucets are fully open. Someone may have partially closed a valve during other home work. The machine needs full household pressure; a half-open valve mimics a failed inlet valve.
  • Inspect the inlet hose screens. Turn off the faucets, unscrew the hoses at the machine, and look at the small mesh screens inside the inlet ports. Sediment slowly clogs them and throttles flow. Rinse gently — do not remove the screens.
  • Check for kinked hoses. Pushing a washer too close to the wall crushes the fill hoses. Pull the machine out and inspect the full run.
  • Verify water pressure at the tap. Disconnect the hose, point it into a bucket, and open the faucet briefly. A trickle means a household plumbing problem, not an appliance problem.
  • If all of the above pass, suspect the inlet valve. The valve can fail electrically or internally. It is a common, inexpensive part [EST $20–45] and testable with a multimeter for coil continuity.

Typical time: 15–40 minutes for the external checks [EST]

5C / SC / 5E (also SE)

Drain error — water did not drain within the allowed time [SPEC].

The washer times the drain phase. A slow or blocked drain overruns the timer and triggers the code. The single most common root cause on Samsung front-loaders is a clogged drain pump filter.

  • Clean the drain pump filter. Behind the lower front access panel (flip the panel down or remove one screw) is a twist-out filter cap. Place towels first — a liter or more of water will exit. Coins, hair pins, lint, and label stickers live here. This alone fixes a large share of 5C codes.
  • Check the drain hose run. Look for kinks along the hose and confirm it is not pushed too far into the standpipe (more than ~15 cm / 6 in can siphon or restrict).
  • Confirm the standpipe is not clogged. Disconnect the hose and run water into the pipe from a bucket. If it backs up, the blockage is plumbing, not the washer.
  • Listen to the pump. Start a spin/drain cycle. A humming pump with no flow suggests a jammed impeller; a silent pump suggests the pump or its control feed has failed. A replacement pump is a common mid-price part [EST $25–60].
  • Freeze check (cold climates). A frozen discharge hose in winter produces the identical code; warm it before replacing anything.

Typical time: 20–45 minutes [EST]

SUD / 5D (also SD)

Excess suds detected [SPEC].

A sensor detects foam formation. Regular detergent in an HE machine foams excessively, and foam cushions the drum, cleans worse, and can force water where it should not go.

  • Switch to HE detergent only. If the carton does not say HE (high-efficiency), it is the wrong detergent for this machine. This is the definitive fix for recurring SUD codes.
  • Cut the dose. Even with HE detergent, most people overdose. Half the label recommendation is usually enough in soft-water areas.
  • Clear existing suds. Run a Rinse+Spin cycle (no detergent, no clothes) once or twice until no foam is visible through the door.
  • Check the drain path too. Slow draining keeps soap in the tub longer and compounds suds — clean the pump filter per the 5C checks above.

Typical time: 5 minutes of work plus cycle runtime [EST]

Ub / U6 (also UE)

Unbalanced load during spin [SPEC].

Before the high-speed spin the machine senses load distribution. One heavy wet item flung against the drum wall will walk the machine across the floor or damage the suspension, so the controller refuses to spin and asks you to rebalance.

  • Redistribute the load. Open the door, untangle, and spread items around the drum evenly. Re-start the spin.
  • Wash bulky items alone. A comforter or rug mixed with small items guarantees imbalance. Wash large items by themselves.
  • Verify leveling. All four feet should sit firmly on the floor with no rock. Adjust the front leveling feet and re-lock the lock nuts.
  • Persistent imbalance on every load. Points to a worn suspension rod or damper — a bigger job; note your exact model number before ordering parts.

Typical time: 5–10 minutes [EST]

dS / d5 / FL / dE

Door lock fault — the door is not locking or the lock switch is not reporting locked [SPEC].

Front-loaders will not run with an unconfirmed door lock (safety interlock). The code appears when the controller does not receive the locked signal in time.

  • Reclose the door firmly. Check for clothing caught in the gasket and lint in the latch strike. Close until you hear a positive click.
  • Power-cycle. Unplug for 60 seconds, reconnect, and retry. A transient lock-signal fault often clears.
  • Inspect the latch assembly. With the machine unplugged, examine the door-side latch for a bent or broken actuator pin and the machine-side lock for a jammed slide.
  • Recurring code on every cycle. The lock assembly (which contains the switches) is the replacement part — a common fix [EST $30–70 part, 30–60 min DIY].

Typical time: 10–50 minutes depending on whether replacement is needed [EST]

LE / LE1 / 1E

Water level sensor error [SPEC].

The water level is measured by air pressure in a small hose connected to a sensor. A leak into the base pan or a fault in the sensor/hose breaks that feedback loop.

  • Power-cycle first. Unplug 60 seconds and retry — sensor faults can be transient after voltage dips.
  • Check for water in the base. If the machine seems heavier or you see seepage, water in the base pan triggers a flood switch that halts operation. Tilt the machine slightly forward to check; find the leak source before restarting.
  • Recurring LE. The level sensor, its air hose (clog or crack), or wiring is suspect. This is a moderate-difficulty repair.

Typical time: 10 minutes to diagnose, longer if disassembly is needed [EST]

3E / 3C

Motor-related fault [SPEC].

The controller monitors motor current and position feedback. Overload, jamming, or genuine motor/control failures trip 3E.

  • Power-cycle and retry with a smaller load. Rule out overload and entanglement first.
  • Check for jammed items. Small items occasionally wedge between drum and tub and stall the motor. Turn the drum by hand (power off) and feel for resistance or grinding.
  • Recurring 3E. Points to motor, hall sensor, or main board faults — diagnose with the service manual for your exact model, or hand to a technician.

Typical time: 10 minutes to triage [EST]

Tools you may need

Screwdrivers (Phillips and flat), a flashlight, and towels for water work. Electrical checks need a basic multimeter (continuity mode) — a $15–25 household model is sufficient [EST].

Frequently asked questions

How do I clear or reset the code?

Unplug the appliance for 60 seconds, reconnect, and run a short cycle. If the code returns, the underlying condition is still present — work the checklist above rather than repeatedly resetting.

Which codes are safe to fix myself?

Fill, drain, suds, balance, and door-lock codes are almost always installation/debris issues — very DIY-friendly. Motor and control-board codes (3E-class) can need live testing and are a reasonable place to call a technician.

Where is my model number?

Washers: inside the door/lid rim. Dryers: inside the door opening. Dishwashers: door rim or tub wall. Refrigerators/freezers: inside a compartment wall. Record it before ordering any part.
