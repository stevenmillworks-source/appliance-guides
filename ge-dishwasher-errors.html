GE Dishwasher Error Codes (E4, H2O): Meanings & Fixes

Quick answer: E4-family codes mean water in the base leak tray — the machine is protecting your floor; find the leak before restarting. H2O means the fill was not confirmed in time.

GE dishwashers use different code sets depending on the control platform. On electronic models, E4-class codes relate to water where it should not be; H2O means fill was not confirmed. Always note your full model number (on the door rim or tub wall) — it determines which chart applies.

Safety first

  • Unplug the appliance before any inspection that goes beyond the exterior — capacitors and live terminals can hold a charge even when powered off.
  • Never bypass a safety device (thermal fuse, door switch, interlock). It opened for a reason; bypassing it creates a fire or shock hazard.
  • If you smell burning, see arcing, or water is contacting electrical parts, stop and call a professional.

What each code means and what to check

CodeMeaning, why it happens, and the checks (in order)
E4 / d5 (platform-dependent)

Water leak error — water has accumulated in the base leak tray under the dishwasher [SPEC].

A float or moisture sense in the base pan detects leaks. Once tripped, the machine pumps out and refuses to run until the water is removed — this protects your floor.

  • Power off and pull the machine slightly out. With power off at the breaker, remove the lower access panels and soak or sponge the base pan dry. Towels and a turkey baster work well.
  • Find the leak source before restarting. Common sources: a loose hose clamp, a cracked pump/sump seal, or an over-suds event (wrong detergent) pushing water over the tub rim into the base.
  • Check the drain hose and air gap. A blocked drain path can back-fill and leak at joints. Clear the air gap (the little dome on the sink) and any disposer connection.
  • Verify level and door closure. An out-of-level machine leaks at the door corners; adjust the leveling legs.
  • If the pan was never wet. On some platforms E4 instead flags the flood/pressure sensor or its micro switch — test the float switch for free travel before replacing parts.

Typical time: 30–60 minutes [EST]

H2O

Water fill problem — fill was not sensed within the time limit [SPEC].

The fill is confirmed by the float switch and/or a flow meter. No confirmation in time aborts with H2O.

  • Confirm the supply valve is open. Dishwashers are often plumbed to a dedicated valve under the sink that gets closed during other repairs and never reopened.
  • Check the float. The small dome in the tub floor must move up/down freely. A jammed float (utensil underneath) blocks fill.
  • Inspect the inlet valve screen. At the valve, debris throttles flow; a valve that fails electrically gives the same code (test coil continuity).
  • Verify water pressure. Run the sink tap nearest the dishwasher; weak flow there means a plumbing issue.

Typical time: 15–30 minutes [EST]

Tools you may need

Screwdrivers (Phillips and flat), a flashlight, and towels for water work. Electrical checks need a basic multimeter (continuity mode) — a $15–25 household model is sufficient [EST].

Frequently asked questions

How do I clear or reset the code?

Unplug the appliance for 60 seconds, reconnect, and run a short cycle. If the code returns, the underlying condition is still present — work the checklist above rather than repeatedly resetting.

Which codes are safe to fix myself?

Fill, drain, suds, balance, and door-lock codes are almost always installation/debris issues — very DIY-friendly. Motor and control-board codes (3E-class) can need live testing and are a reasonable place to call a technician.

Where is my model number?

Washers: inside the door/lid rim. Dryers: inside the door opening. Dishwashers: door rim or tub wall. Refrigerators/freezers: inside a compartment wall. Record it before ordering any part.
