Whirlpool Washer Not Draining

Quick answer: Run a drain/spin cycle and listen: humming with no flow means a clog or jammed pump; silence means the pump is not being driven. Then check the hose geometry and pump filter/access per machine type.

Safety first

  • Unplug the appliance before any inspection that goes beyond the exterior — capacitors and live terminals can hold a charge even when powered off.
  • Never bypass a safety device (thermal fuse, door switch, interlock). It opened for a reason; bypassing it creates a fire or shock hazard.
  • If you smell burning, see arcing, or water is contacting electrical parts, stop and call a professional.

Diagnosis path (in order)

  1. Listen to the pump. A humming pump that moves no water is fighting a clog (or a jammed impeller). A silent pump during the drain phase suggests an electrical/control issue instead.
  2. Check the drain hose geometry. No kinks; the hose should form a high loop and enter the standpipe at the height your installation manual specifies. Too deep in the pipe siphons; too high overworks the pump.
  3. Clear the pump/filter per your machine type. Front-loaders: open the lower access panel and twist out the pump filter (towels first). Direct-drive top-loaders: pump is accessible from underneath — check the impeller for coins and lint. Vertical modular washers (VMW): pump is at the front behind a small panel.
  4. Verify the pump impeller turns freely. With power off, rotate the impeller by hand. Grinding or stiffness means debris or bearing failure — replace the pump [EST $25–60].
  5. Mechanical lid switch check (older top-loaders). On direct-drive machines a failed lid switch blocks drain and spin entirely — the machine fills and agitates but stops dead. Test the switch for continuity with the lid closed.
  6. No voltage at the pump. If the pump is silent and tests fine electrically, check for voltage at the pump connector during a drain cycle. None points to the timer/control or the pressure switch misreading water level.

Tools you may need

Screwdrivers (Phillips and flat), a flashlight, and towels for water work. Electrical checks need a basic multimeter (continuity mode) — a $15–25 household model is sufficient [EST].

Model note [SYM]: Whirlpool made three very different platforms (direct-drive, VMW, front-load). The model number (lid rim or door frame) determines which applies — the checks differ.

Typical diagnosis time: 30–60 minutes [EST]

Frequently asked questions

Is it worth repairing or should I replace?

Rule of thumb: repair when the fix costs under about half of replacement and the appliance is under 8–10 years old [EST]. Confirm your exact model number first — it changes both the parts and the diagnosis.

Do I need a multimeter?

For any electrical check (fuses, elements, sensors, switches) yes. A basic continuity-capable meter is the single most useful appliance-repair tool [EST $15–25].

Where is my model number?

Washers/dryers: inside the door or lid rim. Refrigerators: inside a compartment wall. Ovens: behind the drawer or inside the door. Dishwashers: door rim. Record the full number before ordering parts.
