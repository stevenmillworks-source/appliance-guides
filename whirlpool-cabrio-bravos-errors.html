Whirlpool Cabrio / Maytag Bravos Washer Error Codes (F51, F52, Ld, UL): Fixes

Quick answer: F51 is the signature code on this platform — a rotor-position-sensor fault usually triggered by coins under the wash plate. Clearing debris fixes a large share of F51s without parts.

The Whirlpool Cabrio and Maytag Bravos top-loaders share one platform (vertical modular washer with a wash plate instead of an agitator), so their fault codes overlap heavily. Two codes dominate service calls: F51 (rotor position sensor) and Ld (long drain).

Safety first

  • Unplug the appliance before any inspection that goes beyond the exterior — capacitors and live terminals can hold a charge even when powered off.
  • Never bypass a safety device (thermal fuse, door switch, interlock). It opened for a reason; bypassing it creates a fire or shock hazard.
  • If you smell burning, see arcing, or water is contacting electrical parts, stop and call a professional.

What each code means and what to check

CodeMeaning, why it happens, and the checks (in order)
F51

Rotor position sensor (RPS) / motor system fault — the signature Cabrio/Bravos code [SPEC].

The sensor under the tub reports basket position to the controller. Debris under the wash plate, a loose connector, or a failing sensor breaks that feedback and the machine stops mid-cycle, often with the tub full of water.

  • Unplug and remove the wash plate. One center bolt (typically 7/16" or 11 mm) releases the plate. Underneath, coins and hardware routinely jam the basket. Clear everything you find — this is the highest-value check.
  • Inspect the RPS connector. Under the tub, a three-wire connector feeds the rotor position sensor. Unplug/replug it firmly and look for corrosion or water intrusion.
  • Reassemble and run an empty cycle. Many F51s never return after cleaning. If they do, the sensor or its harness is the standard replacement.
  • Recurring F51. Replace the RPS/harness (a documented service pointer covers this repair); moderate difficulty [EST $40–90 part, 60–90 min].

Typical time: 45–90 minutes [EST]

F52

Large item detected in basket / basket engagement fault [SPEC].

The controller senses unexpected basket resistance — usually a true large item, occasionally a sock or similar wedged under the wash plate.

  • Remove obvious large items and re-run. Check for a load item winding around the plate.
  • Look under the wash plate. Same access as F51; remove the bolt and lift the plate to inspect.
  • Verify the plate bolt torque. A loose plate rattles and mimics engagement faults. Retighten snug (do not over-torque).

Typical time: 20–45 minutes [EST]

Ld

Long drain — water did not drain in time [SPEC].

Same failure class as a front-loader drain error: restriction, worn pump, or drain hose problem stretches the drain phase past its limit.

  • Check the drain hose. Kinks, and a hose inserted too far into the standpipe, both slow draining. Correct the geometry.
  • Access the drain pump. The pump sits under the tub. Separate the connector and check the impeller for free rotation and debris (socks are notorious).
  • Recurring Ld. Replace the pump — a common job [EST $25–60 part].

Typical time: 30–60 minutes [EST]

UL

Unbalanced load [SPEC].

  • Redistribute the load. Open the lid and spread items evenly; re-start.
  • Wash single bulky items alone. And confirm machine level with all four feet planted.

Typical time: 5 minutes [EST]

Tools you may need

Screwdrivers (Phillips and flat), a flashlight, and towels for water work. Electrical checks need a basic multimeter (continuity mode) — a $15–25 household model is sufficient [EST].

Frequently asked questions

How do I clear or reset the code?

Unplug the appliance for 60 seconds, reconnect, and run a short cycle. If the code returns, the underlying condition is still present — work the checklist above rather than repeatedly resetting.

Which codes are safe to fix myself?

Fill, drain, suds, balance, and door-lock codes are almost always installation/debris issues — very DIY-friendly. Motor and control-board codes (3E-class) can need live testing and are a reasonable place to call a technician.

Where is my model number?

Washers: inside the door/lid rim. Dryers: inside the door opening. Dishwashers: door rim or tub wall. Refrigerators/freezers: inside a compartment wall. Record it before ordering any part.
