Samsung Refrigerator Ice Maker Not Working

Quick answer: Test the water dispenser first — if it is also slow, the problem is supply, not the maker. Then check for Demo mode, run the test button harvest, and inspect the fill tube for an ice plug. That sequence finds the cause in most cases.

Safety first

  • Unplug the appliance before any inspection that goes beyond the exterior — capacitors and live terminals can hold a charge even when powered off.
  • Never bypass a safety device (thermal fuse, door switch, interlock). It opened for a reason; bypassing it creates a fire or shock hazard.
  • If you smell burning, see arcing, or water is contacting electrical parts, stop and call a professional.

Diagnosis path (in order)

  1. Test the water dispenser. If water flows slowly from the door dispenser too, chase the shared supply: a clogged filter, a kinked line, or a partly-closed saddle valve. Fixing supply fixes both.
  2. Rule out Demo/Cooling Off mode. If the display reads OF OF (or similar), the cooling is disabled and nothing will freeze. Hold the designated button combination for your model (commonly Energy Saver + Fridge for ~3 s, but it varies) until it chimes and normal temperatures reappear.
  3. Empty and inspect the bin. Clumped ice jams the harvest fingers. Dump the bin, break up or discard clumps, and let a fresh cycle run.
  4. Run the test harvest. Locate the test button under the front of the ice maker module (you may need to remove one small cover). Press and hold: a harvest cycle should start within seconds. No response with power present points to the module or its connector.
  5. Check freezer temperature. Ice production needs roughly 0 °F / −18 °C. A warm freezer (frost-blocked evaporator, failing defrost, door gasket) stops harvest before anything else fails. Verify with a separate thermometer, not just the display.
  6. Inspect the fill tube. On French-door twist-tray families (RF28x/RF26x), an ice plug in the fill tube above the maker blocks water delivery — a classic pattern. Thaw it completely with warm air (hair dryer on low, kept moving) and address a dripping inlet valve if it recurs.
  7. If test cycles run but no ice forms. With supply confirmed and the tube clear, the remaining suspects are the water inlet valve (weak or failed) and the ice maker assembly itself. Both are model-specific replacements.

Tools you may need

Screwdrivers (Phillips and flat), a flashlight, and towels for water work. Electrical checks need a basic multimeter (continuity mode) — a $15–25 household model is sufficient [EST].

Model note [SYM]: French-door bottom-freezer families (RF28x, RF26x) use twist-tray makers with the known fill-tube pattern; side-by-side and RS families use different mechanisms. Confirm the exact model number printed on the inside wall of the fresh-food compartment before ordering any part.

Typical diagnosis time: Diagnosis 20–40 minutes [EST]

Frequently asked questions

Is it worth repairing or should I replace?

Rule of thumb: repair when the fix costs under about half of replacement and the appliance is under 8–10 years old [EST]. Confirm your exact model number first — it changes both the parts and the diagnosis.

Do I need a multimeter?

For any electrical check (fuses, elements, sensors, switches) yes. A basic continuity-capable meter is the single most useful appliance-repair tool [EST $15–25].

Where is my model number?

Washers/dryers: inside the door or lid rim. Refrigerators: inside a compartment wall. Ovens: behind the drawer or inside the door. Dishwashers: door rim. Record the full number before ordering parts.
